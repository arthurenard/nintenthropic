# Overwatch Ridge — vehicles

`public/assets/ridge/vehicles.glb`: the convoy's canvas-backed **truck** (a
fictional mid-century 2.5-ton 6x6: closed steel cab, long bonnet, wooden bed,
canvas over five bows, back rolled up, tailgate lowered) and the officer's
**jeep** (a small open 4x4: folding two-pane windshield, flat mudguards, spare
wheel, jerrycan, folded top, radio whip). Dark olive drab, dusty, mud thrown up
the lower body, chipped edges, a plain painted number on the bonnet sides
(`4-27`, `12`); no insignia. Everything is generated by the scripts here:
nothing is downloaded.

## Build

```
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b --factory-startup -t 4 \
    -P art/ridge/vehicles/build.py -- [--blend art/ridge/build/vehicles] [--quick]
python3 art/ridge/check.py vehicles
```

or `-P art/ridge/build.py -- vehicles`. About 3 to 5 minutes on an M4 Max
(Cycles on the Metal GPU; most of it is the bake, and it slows when other
bakes share the GPU). `--quick` bakes at a quarter of the
resolution for iteration; `--stop model` stops after modelling and renders
Workbench previews; `--only truck` builds one vehicle (for looking at, not for
the file). Deterministic: every random stream is seeded (`geo.rng`).

Review renders of the exported GLB (turnarounds, close-ups, a driver proxy
built to the seat numbers, and the 6x scope at 300 m and 900 m at dawn and in
the valley's shadow) go to `art/ridge/build/review/vehicles/`:

```
/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b --factory-startup -t 4 \
    -P art/ridge/vehicles/review.py -- [--views turn,close,seat,scope] [--only truck] [--quick]
```

## What the game gets

glTF Y-up, metres. Each vehicle is a root node (`truck`, `jeep`) at ground
level under the middle of its length, facing **+Z**; **+X is the vehicle's
left** (the driver's side: left-hand drive). Both roots sit at the origin in
the file; place them by their root. Every node below has the same name under
both roots.

| node | what | notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `body` | cab, chassis, mudguards, lights, tanks, the spare wheel | mesh, `surface: metal` |
| `engine` | the bonnet, grille, radiator shell and core (truck); bonnet, grille panel and headlights (jeep) | mesh, `surface: metal`. The visible shootable engine block: ray-test it; 3 hits → steam from `fx.steam` |
| `cab.glass` | windshield panes (both), door and rear windows (truck) | mesh, `surface: glass`, its own material (see below); each pane has 0..1 UVs for a crack texture |
| `bed` | truck: floor, sides, bulkhead, lowered tailgate, benches, bows | mesh, `surface: wood` |
| `canvas` | truck: the cover and the rolled rear curtain, lashing ropes; jeep: the folded top and the seat cushions | mesh, `surface: cloth` |
| `wheel.<n>` | one node per wheel, origin **on the axle** | mesh, `surface: metal` (tyres are rubber; `metal` per the contract list). Extras: `radius`, `steer`, `side`, `dual` |
| `fx.steam` | where steam pours out: the radiator cap at the front of the bonnet | empty, +Y up |
| `fx.exhaust` | the tail pipe's end | empty, +Z along the pipe |
| `seat.driver`, `seat.passenger` | where a seated soldier's root goes | empty, +Z = vehicle forward |
| `dismount.1..4` | truck: on the ground behind the tailgate | empty, +Z pointing away from the truck |
| `bed.seat.1..8` | truck: on the benches, 1–4 left side front to back, 5–8 right | empty, +Z across the bed (facing the other bench) |
| `seat.rear.1..2` | jeep: the rear bench, left and right | empty, +Z forward |

Root extras: `kind`, `wheelbase`, `wheel_radius`, `length` (and `bed_floor` on
the truck).

### Wheels

Numbered front to back, left before right: **front left = 1, front right = 2**,
then 3/4 (truck: first rear axle; jeep: rear axle), 5/6 (truck: last axle).
The truck's rear nodes 3–6 each carry a dual pair (one node, both tyres).

- **Roll**: about the node's local **X**. Forward roll (the vehicle moving
  +Z) is a **positive** rotation: `wheel.rotation.x += distance / radius`.
- **Steer**: the front wheels (`steer: true`, nodes 1 and 2) turn about local
  **Y**; positive `rotation.y` steers **left** (toward +X). Use Euler order
  `'YXZ'` on the wheel so steering is applied outside the roll.
- Radius: truck 0.485 m (7.50-20 tyres), jeep 0.362 m (6.00-16). The wheels
  rest on y = 0.

### Seats (numbers shared with the soldier's `drive` clip)

Each `seat.*` empty is on the floor directly under the hips, same orientation
as the soldier's root (+Z forward). Built to the `drive` clip: seat cushion top
**0.33 m** above the root, feet on the floor **0.45 m** ahead (pedals just
beyond), steering wheel centre **0.80 m up and 0.38 m ahead**, rim radius
**0.19 m**, its plane tilted 30° from vertical, facing the driver's chest (the
column rises toward him). The truck's cab floor is 0.95 m up (`seat.driver` at
x = +0.45, y = 0.95); the driver's head sits in the upper half of the
windshield. The jeep's floor is 0.44 m up. The benches in the truck's bed are
0.33 m above its floor too, so the same clip sits on them.

### Dismount

The truck's bed floor is **1.15 m** above the ground and its rear edge (the
tailgate is lowered, hanging below it) is **0.55 m** in front of the
`dismount.<n>` empties, which face away from the truck at x = ±0.25, ±0.75:
the `dismount` clip starts crouched on the edge of the bed.

### Materials and textures

One material per vehicle (`truck`, `jeep`): base colour, normal and ORM
(R occlusion, G roughness, B metalness) on one atlas — truck 2048², jeep
1024², WebP — double-sided. Plus a glass material per vehicle (`truck_glass`,
`jeep_glass`): tinted, roughness 0.06, alpha 0.3, `BLEND`, meant to be replaced
by the game's glass (reflections, cracks). Meshes are Draco-compressed. The
wheels of a vehicle share one mesh per kind (left, right, left dual, right
dual) and one patch of the atlas.

Budget: truck 15,524 triangles (11 mesh nodes: body, engine, bed, canvas,
glass, six wheels), jeep 7,674 (8 mesh nodes); the file is 1.6 MB with its six
WebP textures. Atlas coverage is about 69 % (truck) and 64 % (jeep).

## How it is made

| module | job |
| --- | --- |
| `geo.py` | the Builder: bevelled boxes, lofts, revolves, sweeps, prisms, mirrored halves; metre-scale "grain" UVs on every primitive; solidify/boolean helpers |
| `truck.py`, `jeep.py` | the models, in an authoring frame (X right, Y forward, Z up) turned to Blender at build; every number the contract fixes is a named constant |
| `wheels.py` | the prototype wheel (tyre + steel disc rim) each vehicle's wheel nodes are copied from |
| `vehicle.py` | what a model hands on: builders per export node, wheels, sockets, the recentring |
| `paint.py` | procedural source materials (Cycles node trees via `art/western/dustcreek/nodes.py`): olive drab with per-panel tone, sun-faded tops, grime in corners, rain streaks, edge wear to rusting steel (Bevel-node mask, convex edges only), stone chips, dust, mud dense at the sills and splattered higher, fanned behind each wheel; canvas, wood, rubber with cross-country lugs, steel rims with lug nuts, lenses; the painted number rasterised from Blender's own font |
| `bake.py` | packs every part into one atlas at per-part texel densities, bakes albedo / roughness / metalness / normal / AO on the GPU with the wheels in place and a ground plane (the wheel prototype alone: it spins), dilates the islands, packs ORM |
| `assemble.py`, `export.py` | the node tree, the final materials, glTF export (Draco, WebP), then `glbtool` strips the Blender name prefixes |
| `review.py` | the review renders (from the exported GLB) |
| `preview.py` | Workbench previews for shape work (`--stop model`) |

Nothing here edits `art/western/`; `dustcreek.nodes` (the node DSL) and
`charlib.materials.final_material` are imported read-only.
