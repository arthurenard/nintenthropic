# Dead of Night — the art

Everything under `public/assets/night/` is generated by the scripts in this
folder, in Blender 5.1, headless. Nothing is downloaded: every mesh, texture,
sign, sky and animation is made here from code, on top of High Noon's
libraries (`art/western/dustcreek` for the town pipeline,
`art/western/characters/charlib` for people), which are imported read-only
(added to `sys.path`, never edited).

| file | what | budget | built by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `road.glb` | Route 9, Miller's Gas & Eats, the motel, the fields, the contract nodes | ≤ 14 MB | `build_road.py` |
| `sky.webp` | the night sky, equirectangular | — | `build_road.py sky` |
| `poster.webp` | the arcade card / share image | — | `build_road.py poster` |
| `walker.glb` | the dead: one body, six outfits, four hair, runner, bloater, crawler | 10–18k tris dressed | `characters/build.py` |
| `mechanic.glb` | the Mechanic (boss): mask, tyre iron | 10–18k | `characters/build.py` |
| `survivor.glb` | the daughter and the owner, a lantern | 10–18k each | `characters/build.py` |
| `cat.glb` | the black cat | small | `characters/build.py` |

## Building

All commands from the repository root. `-t 4` keeps Blender to four threads
(other jobs share the machine); Cycles renders and bakes on the GPU (Metal).

```sh
B=/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender

# the road: scene -> sky -> bake -> export -> review -> poster
$B -b --factory-startup -t 4 -P art/night/build_road.py                 # full quality
$B -b --factory-startup -t 4 -P art/night/build_road.py -- --quick      # fast iteration
$B -b --factory-startup -t 4 -P art/night/build_road.py -- bake export  # rerun stages alone

# the characters (each also saved as art/night/build/characters/<who>.blend)
$B -b --factory-startup -t 4 -P art/night/characters/build.py
$B -b --factory-startup -t 4 -P art/night/characters/build.py -- --only walker --quick

# the character review set (turnarounds, faces, hands, clip sheets, 10/25/40 m shots, lineup)
$B -b --factory-startup -t 4 art/night/build/characters/walker.blend -P art/night/characters/review.py \
   [-- --what turn,faces,strips,distance,lineup --clips claw,lunge --quick]
# a single head, fast: sculpt, game mesh, bake, close renders (walker|runner|bloater|crawler)
$B -b --factory-startup -t 4 -P art/night/characters/headtest.py -- walker

# the road seen as the game shows it: road.glb imported back (lit, fog, beam),
# and the built dead standing on it at 10, 25 and 40 m in the flashlight
$B -b --factory-startup -t 4 -P art/night/build_road.py -- glbreview castreview

# prove the contract (plain Python, no Blender) and Draco-decode with three.js
python3 art/night/check_night.py
node art/night/tools/draco_check.cjs public/assets/night/road.glb
```

Run times on this Mac (M-series, sharing it with two other bakes):
see [Timings](#timings).

Review renders go to `art/night/build/review/` (git-ignored, like the .blend
files and the intermediate bakes in `art/night/build/`).

## Decisions the contract left open

### The two layers of a baked group: how `lit` and `albedo` travel in glTF

Every baked group of `road.glb` (`ground`, `near`, `station`, `west`, `east`,
`far`, `trees`) is one mesh with one material `night.<group>`, a standard
(non-unlit) glTF PBR material whose slots carry the layers:

| glTF slot | layer | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `emissiveTexture` (factor 1) | **lit** | moon, sky, the steady practical lights (neon, the motel's candles, the booth, the pilot lamp) and the streetlight, AO, the wet asphalt's glossy reflections seen from the player's eye. sRGB, **display-referred** (already graded through AgX, Medium High Contrast, and the night grade): show it as is, no tone mapping, no lights. |
| `baseColorTexture` | **albedo** | the surface's own colour, sRGB, for the flashlight. |
| `occlusionTexture` (R) | **flicker** | the streetlight's share of `lit` (0..1). **Not ambient occlusion** — AO is already in `lit`. |
| `metallicRoughnessTexture` | G: roughness, B: metal (0) | same image as occlusion (ORM-packed, half resolution). |

The material's `extras.layers` repeats this, with the formula. The game's
shader, per fragment:

```
colour = lit * (1 - flicker * (1 - streetlight)) + albedo * beam(position, normal)
```

where `streetlight` is the lamp's current intensity (1 steady, dropping toward
0 when it stutters) and `beam` is the flashlight's irradiance (a SpotLight at
the eye along the aim; the scene's `extras.beam_hint` gives a starting
strength). In three.js: a `MeshBasicMaterial`-like shader, or
`MeshLambertMaterial` with `emissiveMap = lit`, `map = albedo`, lights = the
beam only, and the flicker applied to the emissive with `onBeforeCompile`
(`aoMap` needs `uv1`/`uv2` in older three; here the occlusion texture uses
the same `TEXCOORD_0`).

How the flicker mask is made: the lit bake is rendered twice for the groups
the streetlight reaches (once with the lamp, once without), and
`flicker = 1 - lum(lit without lamp) / lum(lit)`, per texel, in display space
— so `lit * (1 - flicker)` is exactly the scene with the lamp dead.

Fog is **not** baked (`extras.fog_baked = false`): the game draws it. The
scene's extras hold the fog that the review renders used:
`fog_color` (`#2d3744`, the sky's own horizon measured from `sky.webp`, so
the far road melts into the sky), `fog_density` (FogExp2 on view depth,
0.0182: 60% visibility at 40 m, 30% at 60 m, gone by ~110 m) and
`fog_ground` (an extra ground-hugging layer: extinction 0.012/m with 2.2 m
scale height, for the knee-deep mist over the fields — optional). The sky
must not take the fog (`fog: false`): the moon painted in `sky.webp` has to
stay visible to be shot. `moon.disc` is the **hit target** for it — a disc
380 m out exactly over the painted moon, 15% larger than it (`extras`:
`radius_m`, `distance_m`); it need not be drawn (hide it, keep it for the
ray cast; or put the camera's far plane past 400 m and draw it unfogged).

### The corn

The corn is its own mesh, `corn` (children `corn.1..3`), vertex-coloured
instead of textured so the game can sway it: `COLOR_0` is `lit` (linearised
display values — three.js reads vertex colours as linear), `_ALBEDO`
(linear) for the beam, `_SWAY` (0 at the ground .. 1 at the tassel: the
bend weight), `_FLICKER` (as above). Material `night.corn`, unlit.
(Blender's own glTF importer chokes on several custom Draco attributes;
three.js decodes them: `tools/draco_check.cjs`.)

### Nodes added or interpreted

- `light.street` is the lamp head; `light.neon` the pole sign's tube;
  `light.motel.<n>` the rooms with candles; `light.booth` (added) the phone
  booth's panel. Each carries `extras.color` and `extras.flicker` where it
  applies.
- `moon` (empty, −Z = moonlight; `extras` direction, colour, strength for
  lighting the characters) and `moon.disc` (the target, 380 m away at
  azimuth −24°, 14° up — low over the fields to the front-left, so it is in
  the play view and shootable; the contract left the moon's place open).
  The moon backlights the road: the dead come at you rim-lit, faces in
  shadow until the beam finds them.
- `spawn.bus` / `spawn.fence` are the **end** points of their clips
  (`climb_out`, `vault` finish with the root at the node's origin; the clips
  start 0.55 m back and 0.42 m up in the bus door, and 1.1 m back on the
  field side of the fence respectively). `spawn.booth` plays `walk`.
  `spawn.fence` is 16 m out (the contract's sightline is blocked nearer by
  the pickup).
- `barricade.<n>` carry `extras.vp` (where they fall on screen) and `clip:
  claw`.
- `calib.1-9` carry `extras.kind` (hanging, fence_post, bus_window_sill,
  booth_roof, …) and, for the hanging jars, `extras.hook` (the canopy
  hook in glTF coordinates).
- `secret.cat.spot.<n>` carry `extras.name` and `extras.vp`; the pickup cab
  spot is outside the 16:9 frame at play zoom (it is visible in the wide aim
  check): use it for the cat's first appearance.
- `secret.phone.motel` is an empty at the office's payphone (the booth is
  `secret.booth.light`).
- `secret.drivein.screen` is a plane with 0..1 UVs, for a video texture.
- `secret.scarecrow` has its origin at its base, `secret.scarecrow.head` is
  a child pivoting at the neck.

### Characters

- **One armature**, High Noon's bone names, +Z forward in glTF, feet at the
  origin, clips in place at 30 fps. Root motion is not in the clips; the
  clip meta gives the ground speed to move the node at.
- **walker.glb has a 2048² atlas** (not 1024²): it holds thirteen people's
  worth of skin and cloth (the base, six outfits, four hair, runner, bloater,
  crawler). One 1024² would give each of them a fifth of a High Noon
  character's texels. The eyes have their own 128² texture.
- **Dressing** (`extras.dressing` on the scene): a walker is `char.walker` +
  one `var.outfit.*` + one `var.hair.*` (or none; with the trucker's and
  hunter's caps use `short` or none) + `eyes`. `var.runner`, `var.bloater`
  and `char.crawler` are whole people: show one of them + `eyes`, hide
  `char.walker`.
- **Eyeshine**: `eyes` is a separate mesh with material `eyes` whose
  `emissiveTexture` is the shine (a sickly green-gold), `emissiveFactor` 1,
  emission strength baked as 1.
  The game drives the material's `emissiveIntensity`: **0 in the dark**, up to
  **~3** in the beam's centre, falling off with the angle between the beam and
  the line to the eyes (`extras.eyeshine` on the material). The eyes are
  rigid on `head`. The cat's are green, the same scheme. The shine covers
  the iris fully and the white at half, so the whole visible eye lights.
  **Beyond ~15 m an eye is smaller than a pixel** (2.4 cm; at 25 m about one
  pixel of a 1080p frame): the emissive alone averages away there. For the
  tell to work in the fog, add a small additive glow sprite at each eye
  when lit (the `eyes` mesh's two halves give the positions; keep the sprite
  ≥ 2–3 px, colour ≈ `#ccff8c` dead, `#8cff59` the cat) and fade it with
  the same intensity. The review renders show the mesh alone.
- **Clip meta** (`extras.clips` on the scene): per clip, `loop`, `speed_mps`
  (ground speed of the walk cycles: shamble 0.85, limp 0.7, drag 0.6, lurch
  1.1, run 3.5, crawl 0.5), and moments: `claw.strike` (seconds),
  `lunge.strike`, `stagger.root_back` (metres pushed back), the deaths'
  `thud`, `bloat_burst.burst`, `swing.impact`, `mask_off.mask_free`.
- The dead walk **face first**: in the idle and the walk cycles the neck
  juts forward and the head tips back on it, so the face (and the eyes) come
  up toward a player at 1.7 m rather than hanging at the road.
- The survivors are living: their eyes are baked into `var.person.*`'s own
  atlas and do not shine; warm skin, clean colours, the lantern's flame.
- **The Mechanic** is 1.15× a walker: the scale is on the armature's root
  object, so clips shared with the walker play as is.
- `var.mask` is rigid on `head` and a separate mesh: hide it and spawn a
  loose copy when it flies off. `prop.tyre_iron` is under `socket.weapon`
  (on `hand.R`); `prop.lantern` under `socket.lantern` (on `hand.L`), with
  `prop.lantern.flame` its own emissive material `flame` the game lights.

## How it is made

(see the module docstrings; the short of it)

- `roadlib/layout.py` — every position on the road in one place.
- `roadlib/ground.py` — one height field and one big procedural shader for
  asphalt, paint, concrete, gravel, soil, grass, puddles and wetness.
- `roadlib/station.py`, `motel.py`, `roadside.py`, `farm.py`, `far.py`,
  `trees.py`, `corn.py` — the builders.
- `roadlib/contract.py` — the contract's nodes, placed by ray casts against
  the finished scene, and the sightline report.
- `roadlib/bake.py` — per-group atlases, the lit/albedo/flicker passes.
- `roadlib/export.py` — materials for glTF, Draco + WebP.
- `roadlib/post.py` — fog applied to review renders in linear light, the
  display transform.
- `characters/deadlib/` — the dead: heads (`heads.py`: High Noon's head
  sculpt pushed toward the grave, with open sockets for separate eyes),
  eyes, hands, hair grown from the head mesh, outfits, the runner / bloater /
  crawler, the Mechanic, the survivors, the cat (its own rig), the clips.

### A note on the western library

`dustcreek.post.read_exr_layers` reads subimage 0 for every layer (the
position pass came back as the colour). `roadlib/post.py` has its own reader
that reads each subimage by index. The western file is untouched.

## Timings

On this Mac (Apple silicon, Metal, `-t 4`, while two other agents baked):

| step | time |
|---|---|
| road `scene` | 11 s |
| road `sky` (4096×2048, 64 spp) | 17 s |
| road `bake` (7 groups + corn, 512/160 spp) | 28 min |
| road `export` | 13 s |
| road `glbreview` | 1 min |
| road `review` (full) | ~4 min |
| road `bake --preview near,corn` (rebake one group) | 12 min |
| road `--quick` end to end | ~5 min |
| `poster` (1600×900, 640 spp, volumetric fog) | 4 min |
| `castreview` (4 shots) | 1.5 min |
| `walker.glb` (13 people, 2048 atlas, 17 clips) | 31 min (`--quick`: 17 min) |
| `mechanic.glb` | 11 min |
| `survivor.glb` | 11 min |
| `cat.glb` | 1.5 min |
| character review set (`review.py`, per file) | 10–25 min |

The character builds are CPU-bound (numpy signed-distance sculpts, the atlas
bake is analytic, not Cycles); the road is GPU-bound.

## Texture memory

`road.glb` is 7.5 MB on disk, but its atlases unpack to about 0.5 GB of
RGBA8 with mipmaps (the 4096² near group and the 4096×2048 ground are 0.26 GB
of that). On a low tier the game can halve the atlases at load (draw them into
a half-size canvas) — the night hides it well beyond the barricade.
